Let's grab boba

Like every design process my workflow is not linear and changes based on what I am working on.

The first thing I usually do is give it a personality e.g., a Senior Software Engineer who keeps files clean and avoids unnecessary lines of code, or a Senior UX Researcher to help me uncover blind spots, ask questions so I am confident in my thinking and reasoning before presenting it to someone else.

For me AI acts as a bridge to understand complex technical constraints without needing to be an engineer, helping me make more confident design decisions:

  • Breaking down scary backend mechanics (like pricing models, calculation algorithms, or system logic).
  • Gaining enough technical fluency to understand where design boundaries can be pushed creatively and where hard constraints lie.

To protect my creative integrity I initially ideate on paper. People usually start by prototyping with AI but I have found that I produce the best work if I first spend some time doing old school sketching. This helps me explore more creatively and feel confident in my direction which is super useful in prompting AI later, bec let's be honest AI is only as good as your prompt. Once a clear direction is established I move my ideas to AI to simulate user personas, run evaluations ("evals"), and stress-test the user journey for edge cases and potential failure points.
